PD Session · AI for Teachers

Can’t find it?
Ask Claude.

Let AI look around a website for you.

Bingling Liu · ESOL Teacher

Kia ora koutou. Today I'll show you one thing: how Claude can go into a website for you, find what you need, and bring back the answer with a link. It's especially helpful if you're not a "computer person".

Zoom has so many settings.
I couldn’t find anything.

Sound familiar?

I use Zoom every week, but the settings page is huge. I didn't know what most things were, or where to find them. Maybe you feel the same.

Step 1

I asked one question

“Which Zoom features help my teaching?
Give me a link for each one.”

原话：Zoom上有什么有利于教学的功能？每一个给出网页链接。

That's all I typed. I even typed it in Chinese — any language is fine. The important part: "give me a link for each one". That way I can click and go straight there.

Step 2

Claude clicked around Zoom for me

Opened
the website

Checked
my settings

Gave me
the links

This is the key part. Claude didn't just guess from the internet. It opened the Zoom website, went through my own settings page by page, saw what was ON and what was OFF, and tested every link before giving it to me. I didn't click anything.

What it found

Things I didn’t know I had

Quizzes

Zoom marks the answers for me.

Translated captions

Students read captions in their own language.

Focus Mode

Was OFF. Students can’t see each other’s screens.

Three examples. Quizzes: Zoom can mark answers automatically. Translated captions: already ON in my account — students can read captions in their own language. Focus Mode: it was OFF; Claude told me it's useful for tests because students only see the teacher. It found more too, but these three surprised me most.

The best part

One click, and I’m there

Focus Mode — turn it on here:

zoom.us/profile/setting?tab=meeting

No more hunting through menus.

Every answer came with a link like this. I click it, and I land on the exact settings page. For me this is the biggest time-saver: I don't have to remember where anything is.

Step 3

It can do it for me, too

Claude: “Shall I turn on Focus Mode for you?”

Me: “Yes.”

Focus Mode: ON

A normal chatbot tells you where the button is. Claude can go to the website and press the button for you. After it found that Focus Mode was off, it asked me: "Shall I turn it on?" I just say yes. (If possible, show this live.)

Not a computer person?

You don’t need to know where things are.
Just ask.

This is my main message. If you're not confident with computers, that's fine. You don't need to learn every menu. Just describe what you want in plain words, and Claude finds it for you.

Same idea, your websites

Works where you already work

Canvas

“Post this week’s homework in my course.”

SELMA

“Download my class attendance report.”

The Zoom example is just one. It works the same way on the websites we use every day, like Canvas and SELMA. You say what you want done, and Claude does the clicking.

How to ask

Say three things

1

Which website

2

What you want

3

“Do it for me”

That's the whole recipe. Which website — Zoom. What you want — features that help teaching. Then add "do it for me". That's the difference from a normal chatbot: Claude doesn't just tell you where to click, it clicks.

Remember

Two simple rules

Click the link to check

No student names

AI can make mistakes, and websites change — so always click the link and check. And keep students' names and personal details out of your questions. Follow your organisation's AI policy.
